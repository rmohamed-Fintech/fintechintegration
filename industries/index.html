Industries I’ve operated in

I’ve worked inside each of these industries, not studied them from outside. The finance and systems problems repeat across all of them, but the details that decide the margin are specific to each one.

Multi-unit retail and restaurants

Margins depend on what happens in each location every day, but most reporting arrives a month late and at the company level.

Where I help: Location-level P&Ls, POS-to-ledger reconciliation, labor and purchasing analysis, and capital plans for new units.

Franchise systems

Franchisors need to see the health of the whole system, including units they don’t own, and growth that strains support functions.

Where I help: Royalty and fee reporting, franchisee unit economics, technology standards across the system, and development planning.

Logistics and cold chain

Capital-heavy, acquisitive, and running on many systems at once. Integrating acquisitions and controlling technology spend decide the margin.

Where I help: Technology spend governance, capital planning, acquisition integration, and cost accounting, drawn from finance leadership at Lineage Logistics through its IPO.

Consumer products and distribution

Costing, pricing, and customer mix determine whether growth adds profit or just volume.

Where I help: Standard costing, customer and product profitability, pricing governance, item master data, and ERP implementation.

Healthcare and insurance

Product economics are complex and heavily regulated, and data governance matters as much as the analysis.

Where I help: Product P&Ls, investment models, pricing analysis, and data governance, drawn from roles at Blue Cross Blue Shield of Michigan.
