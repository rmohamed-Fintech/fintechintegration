Fractional CFO and finance leadership for growing multi-unit businesses

For companies that need senior financial judgment on growth decisions but aren't ready for a full-time CFO. You get answers about which units make money, what growth will cost, and where margin is leaking.

Questions this answers

  • Which locations actually make money after rent, labor, royalties, and their share of overhead?
  • What does a new unit cost to open, and how long until it pays back?
  • Where is margin leaking: discounts, delivery commissions, waste, labor scheduling, or purchasing?
  • Can we fund next year's growth plan, and what has to be true for it to work?
  • Are our numbers ready for a lender, an investor, or an acquirer?

What I do

Store-level P&L and unit economics

Build a four-wall P&L for every location and a model of what drives it, so you can compare stores fairly and see what a good one looks like.

Budget, forecast, and KPI reporting

An annual budget tied to store plans, a rolling forecast, and a short set of KPIs reported on a fixed calendar to leadership and the board.

Capital planning for new units

Model new-unit investment and return, and set the hurdle a site has to clear. I built and presented a $500M+ long-range capital model to an executive team and board of directors.

Pricing and margin

Menu and product pricing grounded in actual cost to serve. Earlier in my career, profitability analysis I built led to eliminating $20M+ in loss-making sales.

Cost control and purchasing

Vendor terms, sourcing, and spend governance. I've diversified sourcing through tariff and shipping disruptions and raised managed spend from 10% to 80% across a $150M+ portfolio.

Acquisitions and readiness

Underwriting, due diligence support, and post-close integration of systems and costs, drawing on IT finance leadership for 30+ acquisitions. Controls and cost accounting that hold up under audit.

Common questions

What's the difference between this and my accountant?

Your accountant records what happened and handles tax and compliance. I focus on what to do next: which locations to fix or close, what a new unit needs to return, how to price, and whether the growth plan is affordable. I work alongside your accountant, not instead of them.

Are you a CPA?

No. I don't prepare taxes, issue audit opinions, or sign financial statements. My background is corporate finance, FP&A, cost accounting, and operations, including remediating GAAP cost accounting ahead of a public IPO.

How much time does a fractional engagement take?

Typically a set number of days per month, agreed up front, with more time in budget season, a raise, or an acquisition. The goal is senior judgment on the decisions that matter, not a second bookkeeper.

Do you work with companies outside restaurants?

Yes. Multi-unit and growing companies in distribution, consumer products, logistics, and services face the same questions about unit economics, capital, and reporting.
