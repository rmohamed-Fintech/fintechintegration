What I've learned across four ERPs

The software is rarely what makes an ERP program fail. These are the things that do:

Data quality comes first

If the item master, customer records, and product attributes are wrong going in, nothing built on top gets trusted. I've owned item master data for 5,000+ SKUs, and I treat it as the foundation of the program, not admin work.

Costing is a business decision

How you cost inventory and allocate overhead changes margins, pricing, and lender conversations. It needs finance ownership, not just configuration. I led a transition from average to standard costing and built standard costing for imported goods supporting $20M+ in inventory valuation for bank audits.

Integrations are where value leaks

The core modules usually work. The connections to POS, e-commerce, payroll, banks, and warehouse systems are where data goes missing and reconciliation turns manual.

Adoption is the deliverable

A technically successful go-live that the business works around is a failed program. Training, reporting people actually use, and clear ownership after launch matter as much as the build.

Common questions

When should we move off entry-level accounting software?

Usually when month-end close stretches past two weeks, inventory lives in spreadsheets, you're adding entities or locations, or reporting depends on one person. Waiting longer tends to make the migration harder, not cheaper.

Which ERP do you recommend?

It depends on how you operate, not on a preference of mine. NetSuite fits many growing multi-location companies, but the right answer comes from your requirements, your integrations, and total cost over five years.

Do you resell software or take vendor commissions?

My recommendation is based on your requirements. If a referral arrangement with any vendor ever applies to your project, I'll tell you before you decide.

Can you take over an implementation that's off track?

Yes. I'll assess scope, data quality, and partner performance, then reset the plan around what the business actually needs at go-live.
